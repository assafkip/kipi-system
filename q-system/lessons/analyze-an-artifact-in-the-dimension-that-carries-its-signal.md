title: Analyze an artifact in the dimension that carries its signal
date: 2026-07-06
---

When an artifact holds its meaning in a dimension — time, sequence, motion, relationships, order — reducing it to a static snapshot silently discards exactly the property you were asked about. You then analyze a faithful-looking sample that cannot contain the answer, reach a confident conclusion, and never notice the gap. HOW to avoid it: 1) Before analyzing, name the dimension the request lives in. If someone asks about how something changes, moves, or unfolds, the target dimension is that change — not any single state of it. 2) Sample along that dimension, not across a substitute. A snapshot, a single record, or a flattened view answers a different question than a trajectory does; pick the representation whose axis matches the axis of the question. 3) Treat a named reference as something to study in its native form, not to approximate from priors. If the reference is temporal, observe it over its full extent; if relational, observe the connections; do not infer the dynamic property from a still cross-section. 4) Sanity-check the match between what you measured and what was asked: if the question is about a property that only exists across the collapsed dimension, and your evidence is a single slice, that evidence is structurally incapable of confirming or refuting the claim — stop and re-sample. The failure is not misjudgment; it is measuring the wrong axis and trusting the result.
