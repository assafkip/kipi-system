title: Gate externally published claims on truth, handling, and credit, not just shape
date: 2026-11-09
---

When a document leaves your control (press briefs, public reports, partner summaries), automated checks often give false confidence because they only test what is easy to test. Apply these practices:

1. Recognize what your gates cannot see. Numeric-traceability checks prove that each figure maps to a source record. They say nothing about a qualitative sentence with no number in it, such as a claim about method, scope, or intent. Format and style checks (headings, tense, placement) verify shape, not truth. Never let a clean automated run be reported as 'verified'. Add a claim-level review: split the document into individual factual assertions, including non-numeric ones. Require each assertion to cite primary evidence or be removed. Have a reviewer who did not write the text sign it off.

2. Make handling restrictions a first-class field that travels with the data. Each evidence item should carry its sensitivity or sharing classification and its release status, not just its provenance (who supplied it). Restricted material and freely public material must look different at every stage. Make the publishing pipeline fail closed. It should refuse to include any item that lacks an explicit releasable status.

3. Derive outward-facing material from primary findings and evidence, never from an intermediate summary. Summaries compress, and compression is where claims inflate. Qualifiers get dropped, and phrases like 'in full', 'all', or 'per unit' creep in. If you find an inflated claim, trace it back. Correct every downstream artifact built from the same summary, including copies already sent to others.

4. Build attribution into the document template. Before drafting, record who found what, who did what, and how each collaborator asked to be represented. Make it a required section that each named party confirms. Check the final framing against the agreed attribution so the narrative does not quietly invert or erase contributions.

5. Before publication, run a final checklist: every claim backed by evidence, every item cleared for release, and every contributor credited as agreed. Any 'no' blocks release.
